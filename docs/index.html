About the book
01 — Premise

A physical law,
not a curve fit

Most analyses of Bitcoin treat its price history as a sequence of speculative episodes. This book treats it as data from a physical system — and asks what that data implies about the underlying structure of the network.

"The power law is not discovered by fitting a line to a log-log plot. It is predicted by the physics — by the renormalization group fixed point that governs every scale-free network that has grown to sufficient size."

The central finding is that Bitcoin's price follows a power law in time with exponent approximately 5.9 — a relationship that has held across thirteen years, six orders of magnitude in price, four halving cycles, multiple regulatory crises, and the full range of market conditions. A relationship this stable, across a range this wide, is not a coincidence. It is a law.

The book derives this law from first principles — from the mathematics of scale-free networks, preferential attachment, Metcalfe value scaling, and the renormalization group — and shows that the observed exponent of 5.9 is a quantitative prediction of the theory, not a parameter fitted to the data.
